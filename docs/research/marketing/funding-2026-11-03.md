## 2. Does K7 fit? Read the scope in the funder's words

Restack's scope, quoted:

> In scope are innovations and enhancements to core internet infrastructure, **alternatives to
> closed online technologies serving a large userbase, and devices used to run and consume these
> in a secure and privacy-friendly manner**. This also includes full stack security, software
> supply chain management, reproducibility and trust enhancing technologies.

and, from the fund's own framing:

> from libre chips to middleware without a vendor lock-in, from productivity tools to
> **'local-first' infrastructure** — and of course including the end user applications needed to
> consume those networked services.

K7 is, in those terms: a **local-first appliance** that replaces a closed online service (a
streaming account) with a device the user owns, running entirely open firmware and an open web
application, with no account and no cloud. That is a legitimate reading of the scope — it is not
"core internet infrastructure", and the proposal should not pretend it is. It is the
*"devices used to run and consume these in a secure and privacy-friendly manner"* clause, plus
*"local-first"*, plus the open-hardware angle.

### Two traps, both fatal if ignored

1. **AI is out of scope.** *"AI-related projects are not within scope"* ([Restack](https://nlnet.nl/restack/)).
   K7 has an assistant that turns a sentence into search filters. **Do not mention it.** The
   proposal is about the appliance stack, not about the model. If a reviewer asks, the answer is
   that the assistant is optional, local, and not part of the funded work.
2. **The proposal must be written by you.** NLnet states that *"proposals and projects should be
   made by humans, not AI"*, that AI assistance must be **disclosed with the prompts and logs**,
   and that work *"mostly generated by LLMs"* is not eligible ([form](https://nlnet.nl/propose/),
   [GenAI policy news](https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20261020-AI-policy.html)). Practically: this
   document is a checklist of facts and structure. **Do not paste its sentences into the form.**
   Write yours, in your English, and if you used any help, declare it in the disclosure field.

## 3. The strongest pitch is not "a cassette player"

Funders pay for work, not for a story. The work that is both **real** (it is missing from the
repo) and **in scope** (deployability, security, reproducibility, documentation) is this:

| Work | Why it is fundable, not just nice | Where the repo says it is open |
|---|---|---|
| **A card writer that works** — `k7.db` and the `k7.card` mark, through the directory picker, on a real FAT32 card | The only way a user's own browser can prepare a card; the spec exists, the writer does not | [`k7-app.md:331`](../../specs/k7-app.md#L331) |
| **The Snapshot flash path on hardware** — flash, verify, delete the Update file | It is the difference between "host tests pass" and "the device can be trusted with a card" | [`README.md:1133`](../../../README.md#L1133) |
| **Device identity** — per-device hostname, optional pairing, a documented threat model; optional TLS on the LAN | Today the hostname is fixed and two devices collide; the LAN surface has no auth by design | [`README.md:129`](../../../README.md#L129), [`README.md:23`](../../../README.md#L23) |
| **Reproducible packaging** — one pinned build, one flash command, a release artifact a stranger can use | NLnet funds this explicitly ("packaging", "broad deployability") | the build is `idf.py` + a local ESP-IDF |
| **Accessibility and usability** of the 800×480 UI and the web app | Listed as an eligible activity ("improving usability/inclusive design") | — |
| **Documentation as a manual**, not a README: build, recover, upgrade, test | Eligible ("documentation … including educational materials") | the README is 1 200 lines of engineering, not a manual |
| **A small test fleet** — 3–5 units for hardware testing | "Out-of-pocket costs for infrastructure essential to achieving the above" | you already have several screens |

That list has a single story: **make K7 deployable by someone who is not its author, safely.**
It is true, it is in scope, it is what the campaign also needs (§18.1 of the campaign plan), and
it does not require inventing a roadmap.

**One thing to check before writing it.** The analyser ships Essentia's pretrained models; verify
their licences allow redistribution and the use you intend, because the fund requires the whole
stack under open licences. If a model is non-commercial, say what you will do about it in the
proposal rather than discovering it in six months.

## 4. The budget, in the shape the form asks for

The form wants four things: total, tasks with rough time, the rate you used, and expenses
([form](https://nlnet.nl/propose/)). A shape that matches a solo project and the fund's ceiling:

| Line | Example |
|---|---|
| Development, 9 months part-time | the card writer, device identity, Snapshot path, packaging — itemised per task with days |
| Documentation and manual | 10–15 days |
| Hardware | 3–5 boards, panels, cards, a pre-compliance scan if you can justify it as deployability |
| Travel | one community/standards event (eligible explicitly) |
| **Total** | **within €5,000–€50,000**; ask for what the work costs you, not for a market rate |

Do not inflate to the ceiling. A €28,000 proposal whose tasks are itemised reads better than a
€50,000 one that is round.

## 5. What goes in each field

Bullets to write *from*, in your own words:

- **Summary (1,000 chars).** The device in one sentence; the stack (ESP32 firmware + PWA + LAN
  bridge); the gap (a stranger cannot yet prepare a card or deploy it safely); the grant's output
  (card writer, device identity, reproducible build, manual).
- **Effort (4,000).** The table in §3, with days per task, your rate, and expenses.
- **Comparison (4,000).** What exists: LMS/Squeezebox, moOde/Volumio, Mopidy, Navidrome, Sonos.
  What is different: the broadcast model (one stream, every listener hears the same moment), the
  card as the medium, no account and no cloud, and an open ESP32-class appliance rather than a
  Raspberry Pi server. What you would contribute back: nothing to those projects directly, and
  say so honestly.
- **Challenges (4,000).** The security model of a no-auth LAN appliance; per-device identity;
  writing FAT32 from the browser; flash-and-verify on a 2 MB partition; 44.1 kHz MP3 encoding on
  an S3 at ~50% of a core; 24 MB of model weights.
- **Ecosystem and users (2,000).** ESP32/LVGL developers, self-hosters, cassette and vinyl
  collectors, people leaving subscription services. Dependencies: ESP-IDF, LVGL, the LGPL encoder,
  Essentia.
- **Background (2,000).** The repo, the measured board status, what you built and what failed.
  This is your strongest asset: the project is unusually honest about its own gaps, and a funder
  notices.
- **Other funding (1,000).** None. Say it.
- **AI disclosure.** Answer it truthfully.

## 6. If today is impossible

Then the target is **3 January 2027**, and the two months buy the two things that most strengthen

Re-submission after a rejection is normal at NLnet; a stronger second application is not a loss.

## 8. What this does not settle

- **A grant is not a product.** It funds the work in §3, not the marketing, not the tooling of a
  first batch, and not CE/FCC certification — check the eligibility list before putting a
  compliance line in the budget; if it does not fit, keep it out rather than stretching a clause.
- **Timing.** NLnet's decisions take months; if income in the next 60 days is the requirement, the
  grant is not the answer and the reserve-list → pre-order route in the campaign plan is (§18.3).
- **The AI question cuts both ways.** The assistant is a genuine feature and a genuine problem for
  this fund. Keep it out of the proposal, and keep it in the product.
